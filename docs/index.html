Overhead

A little clock that tells you where the plane over your house is coming from, only while you can hear it. Free, open source, for the Ulanzi TC001 pixel clock (about $50).

Install in three steps

You need: an Ulanzi TC001 clock, your home Wi-Fi password, a phone, and a Windows PC for step 2 (Mac? see below). No cables, no drivers. About 10 minutes.

1

Put the clock on your Wi-Fi

Plug the clock in. A new clock shows 192.168.4.1 on its display: on your phone, join the Wi-Fi network the clock creates, open http://192.168.4.1 in the browser, and choose your home Wi-Fi.

The clock then joins your Wi-Fi and shows a clock. Reconnect your phone to your home Wi-Fi.

2

Run the installer

Download the installer (Windows)

Open the downloaded overhead-installer.zip, click Extract all, then double-click Install-Overhead.bat in the extracted folder. The window finds the clock on your Wi-Fi and installs Overhead in about 30 seconds.

If Windows asks “Are you sure you want to run this file?” click Run. If it says “Windows protected your PC”, click More info, then Run anyway. Windows shows that for any program downloaded from the internet.

Mac, Linux or phone: download overhead-tc001-app.bin, open http://clock.local/update in a browser while on the same Wi-Fi, choose the file and upload it.

3

Tell it where you live

Hold the clock's middle button for 3 seconds. The display shows the clock's address, then its password four characters at a time (the left and right buttons step through it).

On your phone, open that address, sign in as admin with the password, and type your street address. Done: the clock finds your nearest airport and starts listening.

Already have Overhead?

Updates are easier still: run the installer again, or on the clock's settings page open More settings → Wi-Fi and security → Update firmware and choose the new overhead-tc001-app.bin. Your settings are kept.

If something doesn't work

The installer says it can't find the clock

Make sure the clock is on and shows a time (so it's on Wi-Fi), and that the PC is on the same Wi-Fi, not a guest network or a phone hotspot. Then type the clock's address in the box and press Find. A new clock is usually clock.local; a clock running Overhead is overhead.local. If names don't work, the clock shows its address when you hold the middle button.

Windows won't open the installer

Right-click Install-Overhead.bat, choose Properties, tick Unblock at the bottom, click OK, then double-click it again. (This is Windows' rule for files from the internet.)

The clock only shows the time, never a plane

Open the settings page and check that your address was found (Home location) and the nearest airport is right. Planes show only while the clock reckons you can hear them; the dashboard's “Nearby and recent” table shows what it's tracking. If it says “Can't reach flight data”, give it a minute; it retries on its own.

I forgot the password

Hold the middle button for 3 seconds: the clock shows it again. The password can be changed on the settings page.

I want to start over

Hold the left button while plugging the clock in: it forgets its Wi-Fi and shows the setup network again. Your address and settings are kept.
