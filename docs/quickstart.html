Quick start guide

Overhead

A pixel clock that shows where the plane over your house came from, or where it's going, only while you can actually hear it.

A Delta flight from Denver passing overhead. Between planes it's a clock.

What you need

Ulanzi TC001
The pixel clock (about $40–50). Nothing to open or solder.
Your phone
To put the clock on your Wi-Fi and to use its settings page.
A Windows PC
For the one-click installer. Or any browser, using the clock's update page.

Three steps, all over Wi-Fi

Put the clock on your Wi-FiPlug it in. A new TC001 shows 192.168.4.1: join the clock's Wi-Fi network on your phone, open http://192.168.4.1 and choose your home network. The clock then joins it.
Install OverheadOn a Windows PC on the same Wi-Fi, download overhead-installer.zip from the project's Releases page on GitHub (github.com/MishaTikh/overhead-clock), unzip it and double-click Install-Overhead.bat. It finds the clock and installs Overhead in about 30 seconds. On a Mac, Linux or a phone: open http://clock.local/update, choose overhead-tc001-app.bin and upload.
Sign in and set your addressHold the clock's middle button: it scrolls its settings address, user and password slowly, twice, with the password in groups like K7PR 3XQ9 TMWA (the spaces aren't part of it). Open that address, sign in as admin and type your street address.
Using it

Three buttons

LeftPress: what to show for each plane (cycles the four styles below).
Hold: replay recent planes.
Held while plugging in: forget Wi-Fi.
MiddlePress: switch between Clock + planes and Planes only.
Hold: show the settings address and password.
RightPress: brightness, cycling Auto, Low, Medium, High.
Auto follows the room light.

What it shows for each plane

City
Where a landing plane came from, or where a departing plane is going. The default.
Codes
Airport codes, from and to.
Airline
Airline name with the codes.
Full
Everything: flight, cities and aircraft type.

Long text scrolls. If a flight's route isn't known, every style shows the flight number. Colors follow the airline (Delta red, United blue, Sun Country orange…).

Two modes

Clock + planes (default)
Shows the time; switches to the flight while a plane is audible, then back.
Planes only
Stays dark until a plane is audible. Nice for a bedroom or a shelf you don't want glowing.
Settings page

Everything else is on your phone

Open http://overhead.local (user admin). Changes save as you make them. Some phones don't open .local names: hold the middle button to see the clock's number instead.

SectionWhat you can do
TodayPlanes heard today, the farthest origin, the loudest plane, rare visitors, and an hour-by-hour chart against a usual day (learned over the first week).
Airport weatherTemperature and wind at your airport, and whether the wind has planes landing over you, taking off over you or going elsewhere today.
Home locationStreet address (looked up once) or coordinates if you'd rather not send an address anywhere. Airport (nearest by default) and time zone (automatic).
What the sign showsMode, display style, 12 or 24-hour clock.
AlertsRare aircraft in gold with a sparkle; an optional chime; an inbound countdown with a little plane icon; an evening recap at the hour you choose.
Clock screen extrasEvery so often, briefly show: indoor temperature and humidity, outdoor weather, date, sunrise and sunset, whether planes are over you today, planes heard today. All off by default.
ColorsClock color, airline colors on or off, and a color for other planes.
Brightness and textAuto, low, medium or high; dark-room and daylight levels; scroll speed; try any text on the sign.
Quiet hoursNo plane alerts or data requests overnight (11 pm–6 am by default). The clock keeps showing the time.
SensitivityHow loud a plane must be to show (60 dBA is clearly noticeable over a quiet street); daily data budget; saving requests when planes are elsewhere.
Wi-Fi and securityNetwork and signal, change the admin password, forget Wi-Fi.

Getting the sensitivity right

For the first few days, compare the Recent list with what you hear. If the sign lights before a plane is clearly audible, raise the threshold a few dBA; if it misses planes you notice, lower it.

Good to know

Troubleshooting

If…Try this
The installer can't find the clockMake sure the PC and clock are on the same Wi-Fi. Type the clock's address when the installer asks (hold the middle button to see it).
Updating laterRun the installer again, or use Update firmware on the settings page.
Your phone doesn't open the setup pageStay on the clock's Wi-Fi and browse to 192.168.4.1.
You changed Wi-Fi or movedHold the left button while plugging the clock in. It forgets the old network and shows the setup Wi-Fi again.
overhead.local doesn't openHold the middle button; open the number it scrolls, like 192.168.1.42.
Forgot the admin passwordHold the middle button: it scrolls the password.
It shows flight numbers, not citiesThe route for that flight isn't known yet. Common at airports without a supported flight board; add one for yours (see CONTRIBUTING on GitHub).
Nothing ever showsCheck the settings page: address found? Airport nearby? Inside quiet hours? Planes may be using other runways today (see Airport weather).
Wi-Fi or the internet is downNothing to do: it keeps working as a clock and picks up planes again when the connection returns.

Privacy and security

Stays at home
No account, cloud or remote access, and no internet port opened. The setup Wi-Fi and the settings page are password protected. Updates need a USB cable at the clock.
What leaves the house
Your address once (or nothing, if you enter coordinates), your location rounded to about 1 km when asking for nearby planes, and flight numbers when looking up routes. Every connection is verified HTTPS.
